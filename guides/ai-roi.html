Lesson 01 / Financial discipline

Evaluating AI return on investment

A practical method for measuring AI workflow value before committing to automation, with a worked example and decision checklist.

Start with the process, not a product demonstration

An impressive demonstration says little about the economics of your own operation. Begin by naming one repeatable task, its current owner and its existing completion standard. Count completed tasks, time per task, errors, rework and the time managers spend reviewing results. Establish a baseline over a representative period, including unusually difficult cases.

Define the outcome before selecting a platform. Useful outcomes include fewer minutes per correctly completed request, fewer late responses, better record completeness or less time spent collecting evidence for a decision. Treat quality as an independent requirement. Saving time offers little value if the workflow creates more defects or sends inaccurate messages to customers.

Calculate the full cost

Separate initial costs from recurring costs. Initial costs include integration, process redesign, security review, employee training and preparing representative test cases. Monthly costs include subscriptions, model requests, human supervision, support, monitoring, storage and correction of unsuccessful outputs.

Calculate monthly labor value from the actual minutes saved on completed work, not from the time a model spends generating text. A simple estimate is monthly valid time savings multiplied by the loaded hourly labor rate. Subtract recurring operating and review costs. Keep the one-time launch investment separate, then estimate how many months of verified net benefit would recover it.

Illustrative example, not a PrismBay customer result.

An administrative team completes 100 recurring requests per month. A supervised pilot saves three valid minutes on each request, or five hours monthly. At an illustrative loaded labor rate of US$40 per hour, gross labor value is US$200. The tool costs US$140 monthly and additional review costs US$80. Net monthly benefit is negative US$20 before implementation costs. The pilot should be redesigned or discontinued unless another independently measured outcome justifies the expense.

Run a controlled pilot

Choose a small, representative sample. Include complete records, incomplete records, conflicting instructions and cases that require escalation. Ask the same reviewers to evaluate both the existing process and the AI-assisted process against a written rubric. Measure the percentage of tasks completed correctly, end-to-end time, review time and defect severity.

Define a stopping rule before the trial. A system that violates confidentiality rules, executes an unapproved payment or repeatedly produces unsupported recommendations should not be scaled because its average completion time is attractive. Preserve source material and the evaluation rubric so other reviewers can reproduce your decision.

Turn results into a decision memo

  • Baseline: monthly task volume, end-to-end completion time, quality and current operating cost.
  • Pilot: users, permissions, sample size, exception cases and review controls.
  • Economics: valid time saved, recurring cost, initial investment and measured net benefit.
  • Risks: data sensitivity, model errors, access rights, service outages and manual fallback.
  • Decision: expand, redesign or stop, with a named owner and review date.

Return to the measurement after an operating cycle. New workflows change employee behavior and create new support costs. Recalculate the business case from actual activity rather than preserving estimates from the launch proposal.
